Abstract

The study of non-line-of-sight (NLOS) imaging is becoming increasingly popular due to its many potential applications, including rescue operations and pedestrian detection by self-driving cars. However, deploying NLOS imaging to a moving camera scenario remains an open area of research. Existing NLOS imaging methods rely on time-resolved detectors and laser configurations that require precise optical alignment, making it difficult to deploy them in dynamic environments.

This work proposes a data-driven approach to NLOS imaging that can be used by a small, power-constrained mobile robot, such as an aerial drone, to localize the 2D position of an occluded human and track their trajectory in time as they move.

Our experimental pipeline uses a standard RGB camera and is designed to accurately estimate the position of an object that is hidden from the camera's view in real-world Manhattan environments.

We introduce a novel approach to handle a sequence of dynamic successive frames in a line-of-sight (LOS) video using an attention-based neural network. The method also includes a preprocessing selection metric that analyzes images from a moving camera consisting of vertical planar surfaces, such as walls and building facades, and extracts planes that return maximum NLOS information. We validate the approach on in-the-wild scenes using a drone for video capture and demonstrate low-cost, real-time NLOS with dynamic capture environments.

Proposed Pipeline

Our proposed pipeline consists of three stages. It starts with raw, stereo, and IMU data for VIO. PlaneRecNet generates plane masks from consecutive frames. Homography from feature matching is applied to plane masks, creating difference images and plane IDs. The raw image at i+1 and the differential image between i and i+1 are input to MPP-T and DPP-T networks, yielding outputs X and V. These outputs, along with camera pose, normal estimates, and plane IDs k, are used in an optimization layer to produce the final trajectory.
